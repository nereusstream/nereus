# M1.1b-Q1 NTA1 codec bounds and legality readiness

## Role and hard boundary

This document prepares one evidence-backed proposal for the later M1.1b grill. It does not accept an NTA1 wire,
implement a production encoder/parser, activate an Oxia codec, or change a scenario result. Accepted ADRs and the
normative V2 contracts remain authoritative. Every number and non-NONE policy below is a candidate until the user
grills and explicitly accepts the final table.

Q1 may add only test/evidence-source harnesses, deterministic reports, and a local readiness gate. Nothing from that
harness may be present under `src/main`, published as a production codec, or called by Kafka, Pulsar, Oxia, or another
runtime module. Complete NTA1, K1, P1, R1, runtime activation, V1 pruning, and scenario promotion remain out of scope.

## Readiness implementation record

The test-scope harness and structured evidence are implemented at Nereus
`94881e6749903e65eec63720c15eb200f69362d8`. Fourteen focused tests execute all six legal protocol/profile rows,
policy mismatch/unknown/payload rejection, Kafka 249/250, both Pulsar candidates, pinned classic-persistent name
round-trip, strict UTF-8, unsigned lengths, checked overflow, bounded allocation, truncation, and EOF. The result is
[`READINESS_EVIDENCE_ONLY`](../../evidence/v2-m0/m1.1b-q1/README.md), with zero failures/errors/skips and
`promotionEligible=false`.

No file was added to `nereus-domain/src/main`, no production NTA1 encoder/parser exists, O2 activation remains false,
and the scenario matrix is unchanged. The design therefore stays `Proposed` and production implementation stays
`NotStarted` even though current-source readiness evidence now exists.

The starting Nereus baseline is clean `main/origin-main` at
`f72d12146455fb4c4d359ef50b362329d50674f5`. Pinned source facts are read from

## Read-only contract audit

### Still OPEN and owned by the later grill

1. the exact non-NONE `FrameEncodingPolicy` kind, version, semantic class, and payload;
2. the closed protocol/profile/policy legality table and literal candidate goldens;
3. Pulsar persistence-name/topic-name byte caps and their native-name consistency rule;
4. `maxCellBytes`, `maxIncarnationBytes`, `maxNta1Bytes`, checked arithmetic, and allocation order;
5. whether the v1 Pulsar contract admits only classic persistent topics or also pinned scalable topic domains;
6. production-distribution evidence for existing Pulsar names. Q1 has deterministic boundary vectors, not a customer
   or production-cluster histogram.

## Pinned-source findings

Kafka's pinned `clients/.../internals/Topic.java` fixes `MAX_NAME_LENGTH=249` and the ASCII alphabet. This is a native
boundary, not a Nereus estimate.

Pulsar's pinned `pulsar-common/.../naming/TopicName.java` parses a canonical complete topic name, exposes
`getPersistenceNamingEncoding()`, and provides `fromPersistenceNamingEncoding(...)`. It contains no equivalent maximum
UTF-8 name length. Therefore any Pulsar cap is a Nereus persisted-format choice. The proposed consistency check is an
exact pinned-source round trip:

```text
canonicalTopicName == canonicalize(fromPersistenceNamingEncoding(canonicalPersistenceName))
canonicalPersistenceName == canonicalize(canonicalTopicName).getPersistenceNamingEncoding()
```

No normalization or alias acceptance is allowed. A mismatch fails before binding-ID derivation or backend I/O. The
future production implementation must reproduce this pinned grammar without importing Pulsar into `nereus-domain`, or
the design must be revised; Q1 does not add that implementation.

## Candidate FrameEncodingPolicy table

The smallest candidate reuses the existing minimum NTA1 order and adds no generic payload length or blob:

| Candidate | kind | formatVersion | logical payload | Meaning | Status |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| `NONE` | 0 | 0 | empty | no Storage-Epoch frame compression policy | structurally frozen |
| `ZSTD_FAST_IF_SMALLER_V1` | 1 | 1 | empty | trial only for protocol-declared eligible clear payload; persist actual per-frame NONE/ZSTD outcome | recommended for grill only |
| `ZSTD_FAST_IF_SAVES_12_5_PERCENT_V1` | 2 | 1 | empty | same eligibility, but require at least 12.5% encoded-byte saving | alternative for grill only |

Empty payload is deliberate: the closed kind/version owns all semantics, avoids a free-form threshold blob, and keeps
v1 parsing deterministic. Unknown kind/version pairs fail immediately. If the grill requires a parameterized payload,
the field table, payload cap, length framing, and maximum-size formula must be revised before any codec is implemented.

The recommendation favors `IF_SMALLER` because it has an existing NONE/ZSTD precedent and introduces no unsupported
threshold. The 12.5% alternative may save CPU and temporary-buffer churn, but it needs NWG1 benchmark evidence. Neither
candidate is accepted by this document.

## Candidate legality matrix

The candidate matrix is protocol-independent; profile owns the policy split:

| Protocol | Profile | Required policy | Candidate legality |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kafka | `OBJECT_WAL` | one accepted non-NONE Object policy | legal candidate |
| Kafka | `BOOKKEEPER_WAL_ONLY` | `NONE` | legal candidate |
| Kafka | `BOOKKEEPER_WAL_ASYNC_OBJECT` | `NONE` | legal candidate |
| Pulsar | `OBJECT_WAL` | one accepted non-NONE Object policy | legal candidate |
| Pulsar | `BOOKKEEPER_WAL_ONLY` | `NONE` | legal candidate |
| Pulsar | `BOOKKEEPER_WAL_ASYNC_OBJECT` | `NONE` | legal candidate |

Object+NONE, BookKeeper+non-NONE, mixed zero/non-zero pairs, unknown codes/versions, and any non-empty v1 payload are
illegal candidates. NPD1 attempt compression remains an offload-attempt fact and cannot make the asynchronous
BookKeeper profile non-NONE in NTA1.

## Candidate wire order and checked formula

```text
NTA1[4]
u16be aggregateSchemaVersion = 1
u16be protocolKind
bindingId[32]
u32be cellLength
cellBytes[cellLength]
u32be incarnationLength
incarnationBytes[incarnationLength]
storageEpochId[32]
u64be epochOrdinal = 0
u16be storageProfile
u16be profileOrigin
policyCatalogSha256[32]
u16be frameEncodingPolicyKind
u16be frameEncodingPolicyVersion
frameEncodingPolicyPayload[known fixed length for kind/version; zero bytes for all current candidates]
u8 sealedEndPresence = 0
EOF
```

With the zero-byte policy candidates, fixed NTA1 bytes excluding Cell and incarnation are exactly `129`:

```text
maxCellBytes = max(38, 54) = 54

maxKafkaIncarnationBytes = 26 + 249 = 275
maxPulsarIncarnationBytes = 22 + maxPersistenceNameBytes + maxTopicNameBytes
maxIncarnationBytes = max(maxKafkaIncarnationBytes, maxPulsarIncarnationBytes)

maxNta1Bytes = addExact(129, maxCellBytes, maxIncarnationBytes)
```

Every add uses checked integer arithmetic. A parser first checks total input against the accepted persisted-v1 cap,
then reads each `u32be` as unsigned, checks it against the field cap and remaining input, and only then allocates the
actual declared length. It rejects signed overflow, unsigned values above `Integer.MAX_VALUE`, truncation, malformed or
non-canonical UTF-8, mismatched protocol/name identity, illegal policy combinations, and trailing bytes.

## Name/cap candidates to measure

| Candidate | persistence name | topic name | combined names | derived max incarnation | derived max NTA1 | 100k all-max raw NTA1 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| performance-biased | 4,096 | 4,096 | 8,192 | 8,214 | 8,397 | 839,700,000 parser-cap bytes; 839,500,000 measured legal bytes |
| compatibility-biased | 16,384 | 16,384 | 32,768 | 32,790 | 32,973 | 3,297,300,000 parser-cap bytes; 3,297,100,000 measured legal bytes |
| earlier total proposal | 16,384 | 16,384 | 32,768 | 32,790 | 65,536 total cap | overprovisioned versus the fixed-v1 formula |

The provisional recommendation for grill is 4 KiB per canonical name and the exact derived 8,397-byte NTA1 cap. It
keeps a large margin over normal full Pulsar names while bounding replay/snapshot/Oxia value amplification. The 16-KiB
per-name choice remains the compatibility-biased alternative because pinned Pulsar itself has no cap. A 64-KiB total
cap is not recommended for the zero-payload fixed-v1 table: it permits no additional legal field yet roughly doubles
the decoder ceiling over the 16-KiB-name maximum.

This comparison is not sufficient to accept 4 KiB. The user must decide whether the native-compatibility risk of
rejecting an existing name above 4 KiB outweighs the worst-case control-plane footprint. A production-name histogram
or explicit product compatibility policy would materially strengthen that decision.

The harness measured real legal maximum vectors of 8,395 and 32,971 bytes. They are two bytes below their generic
parser caps because one classic persistent topic/persistence-name pair cannot independently fill both per-name limits.
Typical Kafka/Pulsar vectors are 202/261 bytes, so raw 100k-topic bodies are about 20.2/26.1 MB before backend envelope,
index, object, and JVM overhead. The all-maximum comparison is a capacity stress bound, not an observed distribution.

## Evidence harness and candidate vectors

The completed evidence batch adds only test/evidence source. It:

- construct real current `TopicBindingAggregateV1` objects for Kafka and Pulsar minimum, representative, and maximum
  candidate names;
- encode only through a test-scope candidate wire, decode through a test-scope bounded reader, and compare the logical
  object and deterministic IDs;
- enumerate all six legal protocol/profile rows and all complementary illegal policy/code/payload rows;
- cover Kafka 249/250, both Pulsar cap candidates, mismatched persistence/topic names, strict UTF-8, `u32be` unsigned
  lengths, checked-add overflow, truncation, EOF, and parser allocation-before-validation guards;
- record exact byte lengths, SHA-256 candidate goldens, name-to-cap margin, and 100k all-typical/all-maximum raw totals;
- emit deterministic JSON and Markdown marked `READINESS_EVIDENCE_ONLY` and `promotionEligible=false`.

Small vectors may retain complete hex. Maximum vectors retain length, digest, and fixed prefix/suffix rather than
duplicating tens of KiB in documentation. No evidence result changes `V2-META-003` or `V2-META-004` to PASS.

## Planned local gate

`v2M1Nta1ReadinessCheck` runs non-zero test-scoped evidence tests and validates the structured report. It fails on
failure, error, skip, missing vectors, a production NTA1 encoder/parser class, runtime wiring, Docker use, scenario
promotion, or language that calls Q1 an M1.1b/M1 PASS. It is a readiness gate, not the later exact codec gate.

## Grill questions

1. Accept `ZSTD_FAST_IF_SMALLER_V1={1,1,empty}`, select the 12.5% alternative, or require NWG1 benchmark evidence
   before assigning either code?
2. Is the product willing to reject native Pulsar names above 4 KiB per canonical representation, or should v1 pay
   the 16-KiB compatibility-biased ceiling?
3. Should M1.1b admit only classic `persistent://` topics, or must its pinned-name round trip cover `topic://` and
   `segment://` domains introduced by the selected Pulsar base?
4. Is an exact derived `maxNta1Bytes` preferred over a rounded power-of-two cap, given that NTA1 has no extension tail?
5. Is test-source distribution evidence sufficient for the first format decision, or is a production/name-inventory
   receipt mandatory before acceptance?

Until these are answered, this design remains `Proposed` and production NTA1 remains blocked.
