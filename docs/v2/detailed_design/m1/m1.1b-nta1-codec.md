# M1.1b NTA1 v1 codec, bounds, legality, and exact-local evidence

## Role and hard boundary

This document is the accepted code-level contract for the M1.1b production NTA1 v1 slice. On 2026-08-12 the user
accepted the exact policy table, six legal rows, Pulsar name/domain admission, and exact checked caps recorded below.
The implementation may add the Java-17/JDK-only production codec and validator, exact goldens, a pure-input existing-
cluster inventory boundary, and the O2 aggregate-codec adapter. It may not enter K1, P1, R1, whole-runtime activation,
V1 pruning, scenario promotion, or M1 Final/PASS.

The earlier Q1 harness remains test/evidence-only and non-authoritative for production bytes. Its immutable receipt is retained as historical
`READINESS_EVIDENCE_ONLY`; production goldens must be generated and checked by the production
codec itself. No runtime code may reference the Q1 candidate harness.

### Accepted on 2026-08-12

1. `NONE={kind=0,version=0,payload=empty}` and
   `ZSTD_FAST_IF_SMALLER_V1={kind=1,version=1,payload=empty}` are the only v1 policies. The 12.5-percent candidate,
   unknown pairs, mixed zero/non-zero pairs, and non-empty payloads are rejected.
2. The six protocol/profile rows below are the complete legal v1 table.
3. Pulsar canonical persistence name and canonical topic name are each capped at 4,096 strict UTF-8 bytes and must
   reproduce each other exactly under the pinned classic-persistent grammar.
4. `maxCellBytes=54`, `maxIncarnationBytes=8,214`, and `maxNta1Bytes=8,397`; every derived length uses checked
   arithmetic and the parser allocates only after total, field cap, and remaining-byte checks.
5. NTA1 v1 accepts only classic `persistent://` Pulsar names. `topic://` and `segment://` require a future contract.
6. The absence of current customers means a production-name histogram does not block 0.2. A future import of an
   existing Pulsar cluster must supply a qualified pure-input inventory/admission result; oversize, non-classic, or
   mutually inconsistent names fail closed. A fresh deployment does not require that import receipt.

## Pinned-source findings

Kafka's pinned `clients/.../internals/Topic.java` fixes `MAX_NAME_LENGTH=249` and the ASCII alphabet. This is a native
boundary, not a Nereus estimate.

Pulsar's pinned `pulsar-common/.../naming/TopicName.java` parses a canonical complete topic name, exposes
`getPersistenceNamingEncoding()`, and provides `fromPersistenceNamingEncoding(...)`. It contains no equivalent maximum
UTF-8 name length. Therefore any Pulsar cap is a Nereus persisted-format choice. The proposed consistency check is an
exact pinned-source round trip:

```text
canonicalTopicName == canonicalize(fromPersistenceNamingEncoding(canonicalPersistenceName))
canonicalPersistenceName == canonicalize(canonicalTopicName).getPersistenceNamingEncoding()
```

No normalization or alias acceptance is allowed. A mismatch fails before binding-ID derivation or backend I/O. The
future production implementation must reproduce this pinned grammar without importing Pulsar into `nereus-domain`, or
the design must be revised; Q1 does not add that implementation.

## Accepted FrameEncodingPolicy table

NTA1 v1 adds no generic policy-payload length or free-form blob:

| Candidate | kind | formatVersion | logical payload | Meaning | Status |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| `NONE` | 0 | 0 | empty | no Storage-Epoch frame compression policy | accepted |
| `ZSTD_FAST_IF_SMALLER_V1` | 1 | 1 | empty | for protocol-declared eligible clear payload; persist actual per-frame NONE/ZSTD outcome | accepted |

Empty payload is deliberate: the closed kind/version owns all semantics and keeps parsing deterministic. The rejected
`ZSTD_FAST_IF_SAVES_12_5_PERCENT_V1={2,1,empty}` candidate cannot be decoded as an alias. Parameterized policy payloads
require NTA2/logical schema v2 rather than retrofitting a v1 length field. NPD1 attempt compression remains an
offload-attempt fact and never changes the NTA1 policy for `BOOKKEEPER_WAL_ASYNC_OBJECT`.

## Accepted legality matrix

The matrix is protocol-independent; profile owns the policy split:

| Protocol | Profile | Required policy | Candidate legality |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kafka | `OBJECT_WAL` | `ZSTD_FAST_IF_SMALLER_V1` | legal |
| Kafka | `BOOKKEEPER_WAL_ONLY` | `NONE` | legal |
| Kafka | `BOOKKEEPER_WAL_ASYNC_OBJECT` | `NONE` | legal |
| Pulsar | `OBJECT_WAL` | `ZSTD_FAST_IF_SMALLER_V1` | legal |
| Pulsar | `BOOKKEEPER_WAL_ONLY` | `NONE` | legal |
| Pulsar | `BOOKKEEPER_WAL_ASYNC_OBJECT` | `NONE` | legal |

Object+NONE, BookKeeper+non-NONE, mixed zero/non-zero pairs, unknown codes/versions, and any non-empty v1 payload are
illegal. NPD1 attempt compression remains an offload-attempt fact and cannot make the asynchronous
BookKeeper profile non-NONE in NTA1.

## Accepted wire order and checked formula

```text
NTA1[4]
u16be aggregateSchemaVersion = 1
u16be protocolKind
bindingId[32]
u32be cellLength
cellBytes[cellLength]
u32be incarnationLength
incarnationBytes[incarnationLength]
storageEpochId[32]
u64be epochOrdinal = 0
u16be storageProfile
u16be profileOrigin
policyCatalogSha256[32]
u16be frameEncodingPolicyKind
u16be frameEncodingPolicyVersion
frameEncodingPolicyPayload[known fixed length for kind/version; zero bytes for all current candidates]
u8 sealedEndPresence = 0
EOF
```

With the zero-byte policies, fixed NTA1 bytes excluding Cell and incarnation are exactly `129`:

```text
maxCellBytes = max(38, 54) = 54

maxKafkaIncarnationBytes = 26 + 249 = 275
maxPulsarIncarnationBytes = 22 + 4,096 + 4,096 = 8,214
maxIncarnationBytes = max(maxKafkaIncarnationBytes, maxPulsarIncarnationBytes)
                    = 8,214

maxNta1Bytes = addExact(129, maxCellBytes, maxIncarnationBytes)
             = 8,397
```

Every add uses checked integer arithmetic. A parser first checks total input against the accepted persisted-v1 cap,
then reads each `u32be` as unsigned, checks it against the field cap and remaining input, and only then allocates the
actual declared length. It rejects signed overflow, unsigned values above `Integer.MAX_VALUE`, truncation, malformed or
non-canonical UTF-8, mismatched protocol/name identity, illegal policy combinations, and trailing bytes.

## Accepted name, domain, and cap boundary

| Decision | persistence name | topic name | combined names | derived max incarnation | derived max NTA1 | 100k all-max raw NTA1 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| accepted v1 | 4,096 | 4,096 | 8,192 | 8,214 | 8,397 | 839,700,000 parser-cap bytes; 839,500,000 measured legal bytes |
| rejected 16-KiB alternative | 16,384 | 16,384 | 32,768 | 32,790 | 32,973 | 3,297,300,000 parser-cap bytes; 3,297,100,000 measured legal bytes |
| rejected rounded-total alternative | 16,384 | 16,384 | 32,768 | 32,790 | 65,536 total cap | overprovisioned versus the fixed-v1 formula |

The accepted choice is 4 KiB per canonical name and the exact derived 8,397-byte NTA1 cap. It
keeps a large margin over normal full Pulsar names while bounding replay/snapshot/Oxia value amplification. The 16-KiB
per-name choice remains the compatibility-biased alternative because pinned Pulsar itself has no cap. A 64-KiB total
cap is not recommended for the zero-payload fixed-v1 table: it permits no additional legal field yet roughly doubles
the decoder ceiling over the 16-KiB-name maximum.

The compatibility tradeoff is explicit: Pulsar itself supplies no equivalent native cap, so import compatibility is
not inferred. Before a future existing-cluster import, a pure-input inventory validates every canonical pair under the
same 4,096-byte and classic-`persistent://` rules and fails closed without a qualifying result. That scan is not a
runtime hot-path call and is not required for a fresh deployment.

The harness measured real legal maximum vectors of 8,395 and 32,971 bytes. They are two bytes below their generic
parser caps because one classic persistent topic/persistence-name pair cannot independently fill both per-name limits.
Typical Kafka/Pulsar vectors are 202/261 bytes, so raw 100k-topic bodies are about 20.2/26.1 MB before backend envelope,
index, object, and JVM overhead. The all-maximum comparison is a capacity stress bound, not an observed distribution.

## Evidence harness and candidate vectors

The completed evidence batch adds only test/evidence source. It:

- construct real current `TopicBindingAggregateV1` objects for Kafka and Pulsar minimum, representative, and maximum
  candidate names;
- encode only through a test-scope candidate wire, decode through a test-scope bounded reader, and compare the logical
  object and deterministic IDs;
- enumerate all six legal protocol/profile rows and all complementary illegal policy/code/payload rows;
- cover Kafka 249/250, both Pulsar cap candidates, mismatched persistence/topic names, strict UTF-8, `u32be` unsigned
  lengths, checked-add overflow, truncation, EOF, and parser allocation-before-validation guards;
- record exact byte lengths, SHA-256 candidate goldens, name-to-cap margin, and 100k all-typical/all-maximum raw totals;
- emit deterministic JSON and Markdown marked `READINESS_EVIDENCE_ONLY` and `promotionEligible=false`.

Small vectors may retain complete hex. Maximum vectors retain length, digest, and fixed prefix/suffix rather than
duplicating tens of KiB in documentation. No evidence result changes `V2-META-003` or `V2-META-004` to PASS.

## Exact local gate

`v2M1Nta1ReadinessCheck` continues to validate the immutable historical Q1 evidence and its non-promotion boundary.
The separate `v2M1Nta1CodecCheck` owns production codec/validator classes, exact goldens, non-zero clean domain and O2
tests, JDK-only domain dependencies, Q1-harness isolation, O2 aggregate-codec integration, and scope guards. It uses no
Docker and cannot claim K1/P1/R1, runtime activation, scenario promotion, M1 PASS, or M1 Final.
